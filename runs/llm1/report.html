2 Thinking — why this move

Evidence: the resonator is healthy but mistuned. The sweep already shows a very deep notch (-50.3 dB) at 3.300 GHz, so the butterfly does short the through path properly -- it just does it at the wrong frequency. At the frequency I actually care about, 5.5 GHz, I am only getting -11.1 dB. Diagnosis: I need to move the resonance up by a factor 5.5/3.3 = 1.67. For a radial stub the resonant length is set mainly by the fan radius, and resonant frequency scales roughly as 1/ro, so ro wants to go from 8 mm to somewhere near 4.8 mm. That is a big move -- about 29% of the full ro range [3, 14] -- so this is clearly a "strong" step, not a nudge. I am touching ro only. ri, alpha, Wf and Lc all perturb the resonance too, but far more weakly, and changing several variables at once on the very first move would make the response impossible to attribute. Coarse tuning first with the single dominant variable, fine tuning with the rest later.

This probe returned something far more valuable than the sensitivity number I asked for. Widening alpha by 3.59 deg did NOT move the notch off the 5.700 GHz grid point -- but its depth collapsed from -50.5 dB to -84.9 dB. The cost at 5.5 GHz barely moved (-31.8 -> -31.3 dB), so in isolation this looks like a wasted step. It is not. Two readings fit that observation, and both are good news. Either alpha controls the notch depth directly by balancing the shunt branch into a truer short, or -- more likely given how sharp this resonance is -- alpha nudged the notch centre by only a few MHz, just enough to land it almost exactly on the 5.700 GHz sample instead of beside it. A -84.9 dB reading on a 50 MHz grid means the true centre is essentially on that grid point. Either way the conclusion is the same and it is the one I needed: alpha is the fine knob and ro is the coarse knob. The machine can already produce a notch far deeper than the -70 dB goal; the entire remaining problem is sliding that notch 200 MHz down onto 5.5 GHz without wrecking its depth. Next I need a frequency knob with resolution between ro's 0.31 mm (about 440 MHz) and alpha's near-zero. ri is the candidate: the fan's radiating length is ro - ri, so growing ri shortens it and raises the resonance, and shrinking ri should lower it. I want the notch lower, so I probe with decrease at normal magnitude, -0.097 mm. If ri really is as sensitive as ro in per-mm terms, that is about -0.14 GHz and lands me near 5.56 GHz. This probe measures the sensitivity and moves me toward the target at the same time, which is strictly better than a probe that only measures.
